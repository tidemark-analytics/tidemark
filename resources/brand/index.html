Strategy

What the category looks like, where Tidemark sits in it, and what we say.

What the research found

  • The category is blue and purple. Plausible's palette centers on an indigo (#4B38D8) over dark navy; Fathom, Pirsch and Matomo sit in the same blue family. A warm teal with an amber signal is unclaimed.
  • Positioning clusters on one idea. Nearly everyone is a “simple, privacy-friendly Google Analytics alternative”. Differentiation comes from how you get it: Plausible and Fathom lead as hosted services; Umami leads on self-hosting.
  • Tidemark' opening. It is the one that installs on ordinary PHP shared hosting — no Docker, Node or ClickHouse — and is built to run as a hosted cloud later from the same code.
  • Consistency beats cleverness. A plain system applied everywhere outperforms a brilliant one-off. Hence one palette, one typeface, one icon set, one motif.
  • Design for the smallest size first. The mark must survive a 16 px favicon, an app icon, a social preview and a full dashboard. That is why the mark is a monogram.
  • Publish naming rules. Fathom's brand page insists on “Fathom Analytics” and forbids “UseFathom”. We do the same (below).

Sources: Brandfetch — Plausible (unofficial palette), Fathom brand page, comparison of Simple Analytics, Plausible, Umami and Fathom. Third-party sources are commentary, not official brand data.

Name & idea

Tidemark — a tidemark is the line a tide leaves on a shore or a harbor wall: a record of how high the water reached, kept without keeping the water. That is what the product does for a website. It keeps the line — aggregate traffic — and never the footprints: no individual visitor is followed, profiled or stored.

Positioning statement

For website owners who want to understand their audience without surveilling it, Tidemark is privacy-first web analytics that installs on any PHP host in minutes — or runs hosted. Unlike hosted-only alternatives, you can own your data and your server from day one.

Three pillars

Clear

Readable numbers, quiet design. You should reach the bottom of your data in one screen, not one afternoon.

Private

No cookies, no stored IP addresses, a visitor hash that rotates monthly. Nothing to disclose because nothing is kept.

Portable

Upload a zip to a $3 shared host, or use ours. Same product, same data model, your choice of where it lives.

Logo

Three pier pilings standing in a tide, with the tide line — the high-water mark — cut across all of them at one height. That is what a tidemark actually is, and the pilings double as the bars of a chart, which is what the product does. Pictorial, no letters, no box. It is single-color by design: the tide line is negative space, so the same logo works in ink or white on any surface, from a favicon to a stamp.

Construction & spacing

  • Mark: 48-unit grid. Three pilings 12.5 wide with fully rounded ends, tops at y = 15 / 5 / 19 and a common base at y = 43. One wavy tide line (14-unit half-waves, a 4-unit negative-space gap) crosses all three at y = 31. Heights are deliberately uneven so it reads as pilings, never as a signal meter.
  • Proportion: the tallest piling equals the wordmark's ascender height (the top of the d and k) and the pilings sit on the text baseline. The gap between mark and wordmark is 0.34 × the font size. Never scale the two independently.
  • Box is optional. Use the mark bare wherever there is a calm background (app, docs, print). Use the tile (Trench, 11-unit radius) only where the mark needs its own ground: favicon, app icon, avatars.
  • Color: ink #0B1B22 on light surfaces, white on dark or brand colors — nothing else. In the app the logo inherits the text color.
  • Wordmark: Inter SemiBold outlines, −2% tracking. It is artwork — don't retype it.
  • Clear space: keep at least ½ the tile's height free on all sides.
  • Minimum size: mark 24 px (so the tide-line gap stays visible) · horizontal lockup 120 px wide · stacked 80 px wide. The tile may go down to 16 px for favicons.

Color

Deep-water neutrals carry the interface. Tide teal is the voice; Buoy amber is the signal — used for cautions, the second chart series and the odd highlight (the “tide” in headlines) — never as a second brand color. The logo itself is single-color.

Motif & imagery

Depth contours with scattered depth numbers — the texture of a nautical chart. Use it as a quiet background (login, hero, social, empty states), always at low contrast and never behind body text.

Iconography

Lucide only — 24-px grid, 2-px round-cap strokes, sized 14–20 px, inheriting text color. No filled icons, no emoji as UI.

Photography & illustration

Prefer none. If needed: calm water, horizons and charts in desaturated teal; no stock people, no “growth arrow” clichés, no dashboards-on-laptops mockups with fake data.
